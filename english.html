TOPIC 1: WORD FORMATION PROCESSES
S1 What is clipping in word formation? ▾
ANSWER
Clipping is a word formation process in which a portion or segment of a word is removed to create a new, shorter word. The clipped word retains the meaning of the original word but is more informal/colloquial. Example: "exam" from "examination," "lab" from "laboratory," "phone" from "telephone".
S2 Give two examples of back-formation. ▾
ANSWER
  1. Donate (from donation)
  2. Edit (from editor)
  3. Televise (from television)
S3 Name three types of clipping. ▾
ANSWER
  1. Fore-clipping (remove from beginning): bus (omnibus), copter (helicopter)
  2. End-clipping (remove from end): photo (photograph), gym (gymnasium)
  3. Middle-clipping (remove from middle): flu (influenza), fridge (refrigerator)
S4 Explain the difference between blending and compound words with examples. ▾
ANSWER
Compound Words: Combine two complete words to form a new word. Example: "football" (foot + ball), "notebook" (note + book). Both component words are independent and meaningful.

Blending: Merge parts/fragments of two words (not complete words). Example: "brunch" (breakfast + lunch), "smog" (smoke + fog). The components are partial; parts are deleted.

In compound words, both parts remain recognizable; in blends, they don't.
S5 What is reduplication? Explain with three different types and examples. ▾
ANSWER
Reduplication is a word formation process where a word is created by repeating the whole or part of a root word to indicate meanings like plurality, intensity, etc.

Three Types:
  1. Exact Reduplication: Same word repeated exactly. Example: "tick-tick" (clock sound), "clump-clump" (boots sound)
  2. Rhyming Compounds: Vowel and consonants after it are identical; initial consonant changes. Example: "roly-poly," "namby-pamby," "higgledy-piggledy"
  3. Ablaut Reduplication: Only vowel sound changes; consonants remain same. Example: "ding-dong," "sing-song," "ping-pong"
TOPIC 2: VOCABULARY & ROOTS
S6 Give one English word with a Latin root "ped" (foot). ▾
ANSWER
Pedestrian, pedal, expedition, centipede
S7 Name one Greek root and provide one English word from it. ▾
ANSWER
Root: "bio" (life) → Biology, biography, autobiography
S8 Explain how understanding Latin and Greek roots can help in learning vocabulary. ▾
ANSWER
Understanding roots helps because:
  1. Pattern Recognition: Once you know a root, you can understand related words. Example: Root "aud" (hear) → audience, audition, audit, audible
  2. Decoding Unknown Words: When you encounter "audiology," you can infer it means "study of hearing"
  3. Memory Aid: Grouping words by root makes memorization easier
  4. Etymology Understanding: Knowing that "pedestrian" comes from "ped" (foot) connects it to "pedal," "pedicure," "pedestal"
  5. Morphological Awareness: Combining roots with prefixes/suffixes creates systematic vocabulary growth. Example: "bio" (life) + "-logy" (study) = Biology
S9 Differentiate between synonyms and antonyms. Provide two examples of each, with explanation of the differences. ▾
ANSWER
Synonyms: Words with similar or nearly identical meanings.

Example 1: "Happy" and "joyful" — Both express positive emotion. "Happy" is general/balanced; "Joyful" is more intense and expressive.
Example 2: "Beautiful" and "attractive" — Both express appeal. "Beautiful" = complete aesthetic appeal; "Attractive" = ability to draw interest.

Antonyms: Words with opposite meanings.

Example 1: "Hot" and "cold" (Gradable antonym) — Represent opposite ends of temperature scale. Middle ground exists: warm, cool, lukewarm.
Example 2: "Alive" and "dead" (Complementary antonym) — Complete opposites with no middle ground. Binary opposition.

ANSWER

"a" before "b" (consonant sound); "an" before "o" (vowel sound).
S11 Fill in the blank with appropriate article: "_____ information you provided was very helpful." ▾
ANSWER
"The information you provided was very helpful."
Information is uncountable; "the" is used because the information is specific and known.
S12 Explain when to use zero article (no article) with plural nouns. Give two examples. ▾
ANSWER
Rule: Use zero article with plural countable nouns when referring to things in general, non-specific sense.

Example 1: "Doctors are important for society." — Refers to doctors in general, not specific doctors.
Example 2: "Cats are independent animals." — Refers to cats as a category/species.

Contrast: General: "Dogs are loyal." (zero article) vs. Specific: "The dogs in our neighborhood are friendly." (the article)
S13 Distinguish between countable and uncountable nouns. Provide the article rules for each with examples. ▾
ANSWER
Countable Nouns: Can be counted individually; have singular and plural forms.
Article Rules:
  • Singular, non-specific: "a/an" → "I saw a dog yesterday"
  • Singular, specific: "the" → "The dog in the park is brown"
  • Plural, non-specific: zero article → "Dogs are animals"
  • Plural, specific: "the" → "The dogs in the park are friendly"
Uncountable Nouns: Cannot be counted individually; no plural form. Examples: advice, information, knowledge, money, music, news, furniture.
Article Rules:
  • Non-specific/general: zero article → "Information is valuable"
  • Specific: "the" → "The information you gave me was useful"
  • Never use "a" or "an" → ✗ "a music" ✓ "some music"

TOPIC 5: COMMUNICATION TYPES
S17 Define intrapersonal communication with one example. ▾
ANSWER
Intrapersonal communication is communication that occurs within an individual — the internal dialogue and self-talk. Example: Thinking about a decision before making it, or having a mental conversation with yourself.
S18 What is interpersonal communication? ▾
ANSWER
Interpersonal communication is the exchange of messages and meaning between two or more people in direct, face-to-face or mediated settings.
S19 Distinguish between verbal and nonverbal communication with examples of each. ▾
ANSWER
Verbal Communication: Uses words (spoken or written) to convey messages.
  • Spoken: Face-to-face conversation, phone call, presentation
  • Written: Email, letter, text message, report
Nonverbal Communication: Uses signals other than words to convey messages.
  • Gestures: Pointing, waving
  • Facial expressions: Smiling, frowning
  • Body language: Posture, stance
  • Tone of voice: Pitch, volume, pace
Relationship: Nonverbal often complements or contradicts verbal communication. Example: Saying "I'm happy" while smiling (reinforces) vs. saying "I'm happy" while frowning (contradicts).

TOPIC 6: NONVERBAL COMMUNICATION
S21 Name five types of nonverbal communication. ▾
ANSWER
  1. Body language and gestures
  2. Paralanguage (tone, pitch, volume)
  3. Eye contact/oculesics
  4. Proxemics (personal space)

S22 What is paralanguage? ▾
ANSWER
Paralanguage refers to the non-verbal vocal elements of speech such as tone, pitch, volume, pace, pauses, and fillers (um, uh) that accompany words but are not words themselves.
S23 Explain the four types of personal space in proxemics with distances. ▾
ANSWER
Proxemics: Study of how personal space influences communication.

Four Types of Personal Space:
  1. Intimate Space: 0-18 inches (0-0.45m) — Close personal relationships. Example: Hugging, whispering secrets.
  2. Personal Space: 1.5-4 feet (0.45-1.2m) — Close friends, casual acquaintances. Example: Casual conversation with friend.
  3. Social Space: 4-12 feet (1.2-3.6m) — Professional/formal interactions. Example: Office meeting.
  4. Public Space: 12 feet and beyond (3.6m+) — Large audiences. Example: Lecturer addressing a class.
S24 Describe four types of gestures with their functions. ▾
ANSWER
  1. Emblems: Culturally agreed-upon gestures that substitute for words. Function: Replace verbal communication. Example: Thumbs-up (good job), peace sign.
  2. Illustrators: Gestures that accompany and emphasize speech. Function: Reinforce verbal message. Example: Spreading arms to show "this big."
  3. Affect Displays: Spontaneous expressions of emotion. Function: Show emotional state. Example: Covering mouth in shock.
  4. Regulators: Gestures that control conversation flow. Function: Manage turn-taking. Example: Nodding to encourage speaker.
TOPIC 7: COMMUNICATION BARRIERS
S25 Name five types of communication barriers. ▾
ANSWER
  1. Physical/Environmental barriers (noise, distance)
  2. Semantic/Language barriers (jargon, ambiguity)
  3. Psychological/Personal barriers (prejudice, emotion)
  4. Cultural barriers (different norms)
  5. Organizational barriers (hierarchy, filtering)
S26 What is a semantic barrier? Give one example. ▾
ANSWER
A semantic barrier is a communication obstacle related to language and meaning. It occurs when words have ambiguous meanings or when jargon is used.

Example: The word "right" has multiple meanings — "That's right" (correct), "Turn right" (direction), "You have the right" (entitlement). This ambiguity can cause misunderstanding.
S27 Explain how psychological barriers affect communication with two examples. ▾
ANSWER
Psychological Barriers are obstacles arising from individual's mental state, attitudes, and perceptions.

Example 1 — Selective Listening: Hearing only what one wants to hear. Important messages are missed. Scenario: A manager giving negative feedback is only heard as "you did something wrong" while missing constructive advice.

Example 2 — Prejudice and Stereotyping: Pre-formed negative opinions about sender or receiver. Message is distorted by existing biases. Scenario: If you assume someone is unreliable, you may misinterpret their delayed response as carelessness rather than circumstantial delay.

TOPIC 1: WORD FORMATION PROCESSES
L1 Analyze the word formation process in "Workaholic", "Dictation", and "Television". Explain the difference in how they were created. ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Workaholic (Blending)
Source words: "work" + "alcoholic"
Process: Merging parts of two words; "work" (complete) + "aholic" (part of alcoholic)
Meaning: Person addicted to work. Created through conscious, deliberate coining.

Dictation (Affixation/Derivation)
Source word: "dictate"
Process: Adding suffix "-tion" to verb to create noun. Regular morphological process.

Television (Compound + Back-formation potential)
Source words: "tele-" (distant) + "vision" (sight)
Process: Combining prefix + root. Can lead to back-formation: "televise" derived from "television". The suffix "-ion" is mistakenly analyzed as a morpheme, leading to back-formation.

Key Difference: Workaholic is modern blending; Dictation is standard affixation; Television is a compound that enabled back-formation, showing how different processes interact.
L2 Explain acronyms and their difference from initialisms. Why is this distinction important? ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Acronym: An abbreviation formed from the initial letters of words that is pronounced as a single word. Examples: NASA, SCUBA, RADAR, LASER. Has achieved "word" status; written in small letters after adoption.

Initialism: An abbreviation formed from the initial letters of words that is pronounced letter-by-letter. Examples: FBI, CIA, USA, TV, CEO. Remains as letters; typically capitalized.

Importance of Distinction:
  1. Linguistic Classification: Acronyms are word-formation processes; initialisms are abbreviations
  2. Pronunciation: Different sounds affect how language evolves
  3. Grammatical Behavior: True acronyms undergo standard word-formation processes; initialisms don't
  4. Dictionary Status: Acronyms are listed as words; initialisms as abbreviations
  5. Educational Value: Understanding this helps identify how new words are created in English
TOPIC 2: VOCABULARY & ROOTS
L3 Analyze the following word formation using multiple processes: "Unbelievable" ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Structural Breakdown:
  • Prefix: "un-" (Latin origin, meaning "not")
  • Root: "believ" (from Latin "credere," meaning "to believe")
  • Suffix: "-able" (Latin origin, meaning "capable of")
Multiple Processes Involved:
  1. Affixation: The primary process — adding prefix "un-" to negate, adding suffix "-able" to create adjective
  2. Root Integration: Combining of Latin etymological elements across different word sources
  3. Morphological Stacking: Multiple morphemes stacked systematically — Base: "believe" → "believe" + "-able" = "believable" → "un-" + "believable" = "unbelievable"
Semantic Change:
  • Original: "believe" (verb — to accept as true)
  • Derivation 1: "believable" (adjective — worthy of belief)
  • Final: "unbelievable" (adjective — not worthy of belief/astonishing)
Historical Context: This demonstrates how Latin-derived affixes combine with Germanic roots (believe) to create new meanings, showing the rich etymological heritage of English vocabulary.
TOPIC 3: ARTICLES (A, AN, THE)
L4 Analyze article usage in the following passage and explain the shifts from indefinite to definite articles: "There was a meeting yesterday. The meeting was about budget. A manager presented a proposal. The proposal contained three important points." ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Sentence 1: "There was a meeting yesterday"
  • Article: "a"
  • Function: Introducing new, non-specific information
  • First mention: Meeting is unknown to reader
Sentence 2: "The meeting was about budget"
  • Article: "the"
  • Function: Referring back to the meeting mentioned above
  • Now specific: Both writer and reader know which meeting
Shift Explanation: From "a" to "the" because information is no longer new; reader is now familiar with it.

Sentence 3: "A manager presented a proposal"
  • Articles: "a" (twice)
  • Function: Introducing new entities not yet mentioned
Sentence 4: "The proposal contained three important points"
  • Article: "the"
  • Function: Referring back to the proposal from previous sentence
Pattern Demonstrated:
  1. Initial mention = indefinite article (a/an) = new, non-specific
  2. Subsequent mentions = definite article (the) = known, specific
  3. General statements = zero article = general category
This follows the "Given-New Distinction" principle: Old information takes definite article; new information takes indefinite article.
TOPIC 4: PREPOSITIONS
L5 Differentiate between the prepositions "in", "at", and "on" by explaining their uses with examples for each time and place. ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
"AT"
  • Time: Specific point in time. Example: "Meet me at 6 PM"
  • Place: Specific location/address. Example: "He is at school"
"IN"
  • Time: Duration; period containing the point. Example: "I will return in one hour", "She was born in 1990"
  • Place: Enclosed space; inside something. Example: "The book is in the drawer"
"ON"
  • Time: Specific day or date. Example: "The meeting is on Monday"
  • Place: Surface; touching a surface. Example: "The pen is on the desk"
Comparative Examples:
  • Time: "I'll see you at 5 PM" (exact moment) vs. "I'll see you in 5 minutes" (duration) vs. "I'll see you on Friday" (specific day)
  • Place: "Meet me at the station" (destination) vs. "The station is in the city" (within) vs. "The ticket office is on the platform" (surface)
TOPIC 5: COMMUNICATION TYPES
L6 Explain the concept of mass communication and contrast it with public communication. ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Mass Communication
  • Definition: Process by which messages are produced and distributed to large, anonymous, heterogeneous audiences through media channels
  • Technology-mediated (newspaper, TV, radio, internet)
  • Large, geographically dispersed audience
  • One-directional flow (sender → many receivers)
  • Delayed/limited feedback (letters to editor, comments, ratings)
  • Example: News broadcast on television reaches millions simultaneously

TOPIC 6: NONVERBAL COMMUNICATION
L7 Analyze how paralanguage elements can change the meaning of the same spoken words with specific examples. ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Example Sentence: "That's really great."

Variation 1: Fast pace, high pitch, loud volume → Genuine excitement, enthusiasm, sincerity
Variation 2: Slow pace, low pitch, soft volume → Sarcasm, criticism, disapproval
Variation 3: Rising tone at end, hesitant pause → Uncertainty, questioning, lack of confidence
Variation 4: Flat tone, monotone, neutral pace → Indifference, emotional detachment

Detailed Analysis:
ElementHigh/PositiveLow/Negative
PitchExcitementAuthority, seriousness
VolumeConfidenceIntimacy, uncertainty
PaceAnxiety, enthusiasmThoughtfulness, importance
ToneRising = questionFalling = certainty
PausesStrategic = emphasisFrequent = hesitation
Real-world Application: In job interviews, the same answer "I completed the project on time" means differently — confident delivery (steady pace, clear pitch, strong volume) = Competent, reliable; hesitant delivery (slow pace, rising pitch, soft volume) = Unsure, lacking confidence. This demonstrates that how we say something is often more important than what we say.
TOPIC 7: COMMUNICATION BARRIERS
L8 Analyze the following communication breakdown and identify the barriers involved. Suggest solutions. Scenario: In a multinational office, an Indian manager tells their American team: "We should think about this more." The American team immediately proceeds with their plan, considering the statement as hesitant agreement. Later, the manager is upset because "think about this more" meant "stop and reconsider completely." ▾
DETAILED ANSWER
Barriers Identified: 1. Cultural Barrier
  • High-context (Indian) vs. Low-context (American) communication styles
  • High-context cultures (India, Japan) communicate indirectly; implied meanings are important
  • Low-context cultures (USA, Germany) prefer explicit, direct communication
  • Manager's indirect "We should think..." is understood as hesitation by direct-communication culture
2. Semantic/Language Barrier
  • Ambiguity in word choice
  • "Think about this more" is unclear — doesn't specify if it means "think for 5 minutes" or "reconsider entirely"
3. Organizational Barrier
  • Cross-cultural communication in multinational setting
  • No protocol for clarifying intent in cross-cultural communication
4. Psychological Barrier
  • Lack of awareness of cultural differences
  • Team assumes their communication style is universal
Solutions:
  • Immediate: Manager should be more explicit. Team should ask clarifying questions.
  • Short-term: Organizational training on cross-cultural communication. Establish team norms for clarity. Use written summaries after meetings.
  • Long-term: Create organizational guidelines for communication in multicultural teams. Develop awareness that "clear communication" is culturally defined. Implement feedback systems.
Key Learning: Effective communication in multicultural contexts requires awareness that directness vs. indirectness is cultural, not universal.
